White Mesa Fat Ass 50k

Not a proper race report

What follows is what I sent some friends. They all follow me on Strava, so they had access to the photos and videos I posted for this activity.

Here is what I sent (spelling errors and all)

I already sent this to [REDACTED] and our mutual friend [REDACTED]. I then sent it to [REDACTED] with this disclaimer (which also applies to you)˘: It’s ridiculously long and covers a race you’re unfamiliar with and probably will remain uninterested in, so you probably want to skim it at most, but since we were texting about itk here it is:

I apologize for the length and lack of editing. I can’t justify the time to do a proper race report.

I was watching the weather report for San Ysidro and I decided I sort of didn’t want people coming out for WMFA50k. In fact, until yesterday morning, my plan was to drive my van to my house, pick up my truck (with 4WD) and take it. However, when I got up yesterday morning, the predicted amount of rain was just small enough that I decided to risk taking the van, but I did bring my 0F sleeping bag so that if I got stuck for a day or so, I’d be warm while I ate random aid station food and drank from my 7 gallon container.

The first thing I did in the morning was start water boiling on the stove so I could fill three thermoses. When it came time to pour water into the thermoses, I knew I could spill boiling water on myself, but I told myself that I’ve done this before. My guess is I did it before with pants on, because yes, I did get boiling water on my legs. Oops.

The drive to the race wasn’t too bad. There were some sketchy portions where I paid close attention to wheel placement. My van is only two wheel drive and although the tires aren’t bald bald, they have little tread. There was one particular place that I dreaded hitting on the way back, especially if rain made the road worse.

A little before 8pm, a guy pulls in. He seemed familiar, but I couldn’t place his name. He’’s Derek and he was going to do just one loop. Whew! With luck he’ll be done before the rain. Well, we got a little sprinkling on the first loop, but it was the second and third that had the real nasty rain. The storm on the second actually started with hail and sleet. Whee!

I took a lot of videos and pictures. I didn’t, however, capture the worst of the storm. I simply didn’t want to get my camera out from where I had been keeping it, which is in a zippered pocket of my Adv Skin 12, with my rain vest around me and the ultravest. I don’t second guess that decision, but the sleet and hail would have been nice to document and the ridiculous wind (100% tailwind!) that came with the lap three storm would have made for special footage.

On my fourth lap, I saw a horse. I talked to it and it came over. I gave it a gel. The only ones I had were vanilla bean with caffeine. I have since looked it up and seen that I should not have given the horse a gel, but I did. I hope it did not harm (and my guess is that it didn’t), seeing how it was 20mg. I emptied the gel into my hand and the horse ate it. I then emptied a second and the horse decided it didn’t want the second one. OK, so what do I do with a handful of gel? I ate it myself, but it was a bit messy and I got some in my beard and of course my hand was sticky for the remainder of the run. I was reluctant to get my gloves sticky, so I kept them off for the remainder of the run, too.

In addition to rain during the course making things harder, the recent rains seemed to have made the course a little harder in that I think there was more erosion which left more embedded rocks to trip on. On my first lap, I tripped and hit the ground at least twice. FTR, I often trip on that course, but I was aware that there would be essentially nobody out there (other than Derek, and he was only doing one lap), so I really had to be careful to not injure myself. In addition to the erosion causing more of those little rock spikes, I think that some of the slightly softer soil / wash was gone, making some of the hard-packed sections just slightly harder, although perhaps I’m imagining things because there was much less tortoise-shell slick rock at Deadman Peaks this year due to us only using the first nine miles of the course.

I have been out on that course a few times after recent rains and it’s prey to surprise hidden mud, where there’s mud just below the surface, but the surface appears dry. The mud underneath is sticky, but not too slippery, unless it’s on an incline (or on the sides of a grooved path). There was no surprise mud on my first lap. Yay. However, the remaining three had mud, and even though it had been raining and you’d think it would just be obvious mud, I did get surprise mud here and there. I did not, however, fall to the ground due to it, although I came close a few times and I simply did a lot more walking than normal.

In addition to doing a lot of walking, I stopped to add or remove rain gear quite a bit. I brought my Ultimate Directions ultra taped-seams lightweight rain jacket and some fairly serious Marmot rain pants. I had not used the rain pants in years, and I had forgotten that they were slightly torn, but even worse than that is the waist was too big. I bought them before I lost all that weight in 2018. D’oh! I wound up holding them together with one hand when I ran or would have to hike them up every once in a while when I hiked. It was comically annoying.

The sun only came out here and there. By the time I finished my third loop it looked like it was going to be cloudy due to where the sun was, but probably not rain more, so I stopped and put tights on (although it’s rare that I run with tights, I actually have four different thicknesses of tights, mostly for biking) and changed into dry socks. I had started out with longish Smartwoool. I changed into WSER Dry Max socks. I had brought a single 12 oz. 5% Ex Novo Mexican Lager beer and decided to drink it between my third and fourth lap so I’d be completely sober for the drive back. By then I knew it was going to be a bitch.

I stuffed my remaining Actik headlamp and a Kogolla light bar with BP-2 into my vest because it was clear I’d be finishing in the dark. I used to have two Actiks but I appear to have lost one at Deadman Peaks. I haven’t thoroughly checked my van. After I do so, if I still don’t have it, I’ll ask Eric if he found it.

The temperature was nice for running, but I didn’t do much of that on my fourth lap. I was super concerned with injuring myself. It turns out that during one brief spell of good weather I did see a family of five out near the trailhead. Presumably a mother and father with three little kids. Other than Derek (whom I didn’t see much of on the trail in part because he didn’t do the course a James Breyfogle laid it out – he took the dirt road and didn’t go over the hills that have the crazy geologic features. I actually saw him from a distance and headed toward him so I could show him the big one (which is just slightly off the official route), but unbeknownst to me, he was running on the dirt road and so he just happened to pass me on the section that I never ran onk so we never saw each other.

Like so many of my other races this year, I packed at the last minute. I had set a few things aside from DPTR that I knew I’d use at WMFA, but I didn’t actually grab my ARR bags and start filling them until 5:15am yesterday morning. My plan was to have everything in my van so I could hand mom her coffee at 6am and be off and that’s what I did, but I forgot to bring chemical hand-warmers and a cable to connect my BP-2 to my phone. I didn’t think of the latter until I as in my third lap and I saw just how much I had depleted my battery (in part due to me Facetime Videoing a non-runner friend of mine), but I realized that I had a spare cable in the van. Unfortunately, I forgot to get that cable during my extended break between laps three and four.

Pretty much the entire run was in Whee! mode, but I did have some aches and pains. My left pinky is not fully recovered. The bump I got at Glorieta last year is still there and was giving me some trouble here and there. I had some GI issues which also caused me to have to pee quite a bit which is slightly tricky with the tights. I didn’t take any ibuprofen and that’s the longest I’ve been on my feet without ibuprofen in a long while. My feet are more tender than I’d like, but are actually fairly tough. I considered using Salty Britches, but I don’t have a lot left so I chose not to, and my feet held up fine.

The dirt road back to 550 was white knuckle, with some portions more intense than others. I could never predict with certainty just how much mud would be underneath which tires. I made many predictions that were good, but basically I knew that I had to keep moving fast enough so that if I hit a patch that my tires couldn’t grip, I’d slide through it to a patch where they can. That happened a few times, but that also meant that sometimes I’d get a surprising side-slip. It was fun, but it was one of those things where I couldn’t take delight until I was done. Finally I was on 550 and …

… 550 was surprisingly fucked up. It was not raining and none of it was washed out, but people were driving anywhere from 80mpg (i.e. 10 miles above the speed limit) to ?40? and there were more than a few cars or semis pulled over and even a couple of police or sheriff’s cars out watching people. I think I started out driving around fifty miles an hour just because I was still spooked from that dirt road and there were wet spots here and there, but as I got closer to Bernalillo, I sped up, but it was definitely weird. I-25 and the rest was completely fine.

I knew there would be some residual wetness today and that I’d choose not to ride my bike, so I said “fuck it” and drank an entire 22 oz. 12% ABV Kirkland Vintage Ale. That’s basically 4.4 beers in a fairly empty stomach. I stayed up a little bit chatting with my Friday and Saturday video friends, often with my video and audio off so I could eat.

The entire time I was gone, Claude was plugging away on some very needed, fairly intense UI work. I had managed to use 99% of my weekly credit by the time it rolled over at 8pm on Friday, and I had it continue. It hasn’t even been two full days and I’ve used 48% of my weekly allotment, but I have a weekly reset that expires on December 10th, so my plan is to crank away hard on the UI and then decide whether it makes sense to use the reset this week or to just wrap up my AI use early, since I’ll be driving down to Las Cruces for Tortugas Friday. I am actually much more excited about work than I am about running, but–as you can see–I’m pretty excited about running.

My last few weekends have been: Mt Taylor, two weekends of ballooning, Duke City Marathon, Day of the Tread century, JJ, DPTR53M and WMFA50k. Tomorrow I start my “serious” training for BMDM and although it’s grueling and really sucks hard at the beginning and the end, I know that if I don’t injure myself, I can get to the middle. Additionally, Friday is Tortugas and the following week has three days of running in a row: Ed’s half-marathon, Perky’s Black Friday Turkey Trots and Eric’s Bosque 50k. By the time all of that is done, I’ll be back in the BMDM training groove and–more importantly–I should have some crazy good stuff happening at work. A week later it’s lottery weekend and the week after that Margaret graduates and is commissioned.

Whee!

Why this “report”

It was there. It’s also the last event before I start my 2027 Bataan Memorial Death March training block.

As I’ve mentioned elsewhere, I have three training blocks a year. Each one is sufficiently different the one that precedes it that I look forward to the next block, except my BMDM training block requires me to push myself fairly hard and that’s intimidating and annoying at first. Overall, I think the entire package (three training blocks a year) helps me deal with stress. I love my mom, but being her caregiver is—at least for me—stressful.
